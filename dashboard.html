What a handoff costs decides the operating model

A target operating model arrives as four columns on a slide — retained, captive, provider, automated — with the activities sorted into them by the people whose functions are being sorted. What the sorting costs is missing. The expensive part is not where an activity sits but how often work crosses between columns, and nobody has measured which activities follow each other.

The answer

Concentrating purchase-to-pay pays only above USD 3.38 per handoff. That sits inside the range this study is willing to defend — near its top edge.

Below that price the model spreads work across cheaper cities and pays for the crossings; at it, 22 of 29 activities change unit at once. The case for concentrating this process therefore rests on the upper half of what a handoff can defensibly be argued to cost, not on the middle of it.

This is not a target operating model, and the tool below does not name one. Cost decides 13 of the 29 positions. The other 16 are ties, and they are left undecided rather than filled in.

Note: the contact requirement is only that somebody can answer a supplier inside their working day, and on this process it is worth more than the entire labour-arbitrage argument. Read the two columns against each other: segregation of duties moves three activities and costs about USD 409k, so it is the cheapest constraint to state and among the most expensive to obey — the count of activities moved is not a proxy for what a constraint costs. None of the four is decorative, which is a weaker claim than it sounds and worth making anyway; the usual outcome of this test is that two of them do nothing.

Exhibit 5 · what the model is standing on

One input is measured; the four that decide the cost are declared

  • Measured. Which activities follow each other, from 1,344,189 transitions across 11,973 variants of a real SAP log. 210,536 of those are an activity repeating and never cross a unit; 452,301 touch a vendor or a system step and are unavoidable whatever anyone decides. Both are excluded, so 681,352 transitions are priced.
  • Declared. Handling time per activity, the mapping from activity to wage group, the provider margin, and the coordination price itself. All four are resampled 10,000 times, and 7 of 29 activities hold their unit in at least 75% of 10,000 draws.
  • Measured, and it failed. The transactional/judgment classifier ported from the sibling study is 79% accurate against the hand-labelled gold set, with the ported keyword stage deciding 17% of names and recalling 0% of judgment work. Its error is what the resampling runs over, rather than an assumed rate.
  • Not separable. India has no city-level wage in any reachable source, so four of its five cities are identical on every input the model reads. They are reported as one band, not ranked.

Note: purchase-to-pay only. Nothing here extends to record-to-report or order-to-cash, and a narrow model that was checked beats a broad one that was asserted. Transition and severance costs, attrition, tax and incentives are all absent: the comparison is steady state to steady state.

Solver: OR-Tools CP-SAT, solved to proven optimality at every stop on the control. No language model anywhere in the assignment decision. Inputs from p2p-process-mining and gbs-location-selection; the segregation-of-duties rule from finance-close-control-agent. Both pre-registered metrics, the configuration and the full method are in the repository.
